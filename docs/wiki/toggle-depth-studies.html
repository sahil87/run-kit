Toggle Depth Studies

How a latched ("on") toggle button should look — pressed in rather than lit up with a border. Specimens are the five latching families that share one arm today: the sidebar section rail, the top-bar icon square, the bottom-bar kbd chips, the flush surface-toggle segments, and a tile-header verb. Every button is live — click to flip it. Toggle theme and pointer in the toolbar; hover the buttons for the hover arm.

0 · Today, annotated

The screenshot: two of the four section-rail toggles are on. The shipped latch (LATCHED_ARM_RINGED in control.tsx) is 1 a 1px ring-inset in accent-green, 2 a 15% green wash, 3 green glyph ink. Hover deepens the wash to 25%. It is the one latch algebra every latching control composes — bordered controls paint the ring on their real border (LATCHED_ARM), flush segments drop the border axis (LATCHED_ARM_FLUSH).

Why it looks like this: the control contract settled scheme C — accent-green means state, never hover. Hover is hue-free brightness; a green control always reads "on". The ring-inset was chosen over a real border so latching never shifts layout. Both of those decisions survive every option below; what changes is the shape of the on-state — an outline that lights up (additive) versus a well the button sits in (subtractive).

Section rail as shipped

1ring · 2wash · 3ink — three additive channels on one 24px box. Four adjacent toggles with two on read as two lit LEDs; the eye is drawn to the on-state's edge rather than the glyph.

What "pressed in" needs

A latched key in the physical world is lower than its neighbours, darker at the top edge (it sits in shadow), and carries no outline of its own — the outline belongs to the hole. Three CSS channels map onto that: ground = --color-bg-inset (the recessed-well token the switch-off track and marker wells already use), an inset box-shadow for the top-edge shade, and no ring. The open question is whether the glyph ink stays green (state hue) or goes neutral (depth alone).

1 · Seven treatments, five families each

Each card runs one on-state recipe across all five latching families. The rail has Boards + Server on; the top bar has autofit on; the chips have ⌃ latched; the segments have web open; the tile verb is the find bar (open). Readouts show the glyph-ink : ground contrast ratio for the on-state in the current theme (WCAG 1.4.11 asks ≥ 3:1 for non-text UI).

A · Today — ring + wash + green ink

baseline
For: unambiguous on-state, one algebra everywhere, no layout shift. Against: reads as lit, not pressed; the ring competes with the focus ring for the "outline" channel (they differ only by offset); on the rail, two lit boxes out of four dominate the row.

B · Well — depth only, neutral ink

depth alone
For: the purest "key is down" reading; hue stays free for status. Against: breaks scheme C's promise that a latched control is green, so on now depends on a 1–2px shadow — weak at a glance on a busy sidebar, invisible in a screenshot at 50% zoom, and on light themes bg-inset is one step from the chrome ground so the shadow carries everything.

C · Well + green ink — on the existing tokens

dark onlydepth = pressed · hue = state
For: the button visibly drops into the ground (what you asked for) while the glyph keeps the green that scheme C reserves for state — two independent channels agree, so it survives both a colour-blind read (depth) and a squint read (hue). The wash and the ring go away; the ink stays exactly where today's contrast budget already lives. Against: two shadow tokens to theme (--well-shadow per theme category); on pure-black palettes bg-inset clamps to the ground, so the well needs the 1px bottom lip to keep an edge (toggle pure black above). Light theme: fails — bg-inset sits 0.000 ΔL from the chrome ground so the floor vanishes, the accent-green glyph (2.7 : 1) is fainter than the neutral off glyphs (4.0 : 1), and the dimmed border reads as disabled. Every channel that carries the well in dark is absent or inverted in light. See C′.

C′ · Well — theme-derived floor, ink and border

recommendedsame shape as C · light fixed
Identical to C in dark and pure black. In light, three tokens change: the floor is derived as one OKLab step (ΔL 0.06) below the chrome ground — #d6d6d6 here — the same derivation the chrome material uses, instead of bg-inset; the glyph ink is a darker green (#15803d, 4.2 : 1 on the chrome, 3.5 : 1 on the floor — the first light-theme latch ink to clear the 3 : 1 non-text floor); the border of bordered controls darkens toward secondary text instead of fading. The shadow is tighter and ~28% instead of 18%, with a faint 1px inner edge, and the white bottom lip is dropped (a light well is read from its dark top edge, not a bright bottom one). Cost: a fourth palette-derived colour — a green ink variant distinct from the fill/track green — because the light palettes' accentGreen is tuned as a wash and switch-track colour and has never been a glyph on the chrome ground.

D · Keycap — hard top shadow + 1px glyph drop

most physical
For: the strongest press metaphor — a flat 2px shade band plus the glyph moving down 1px, the way a real key travels. Against: the glyph shift is a transform (no layout change) but a 1px jump on toggle reads as jitter next to unmoved siblings, and the hard band looks like a rendering seam at 24px; on the 33px chips it's fine. Better reserved for the kbd chips alone if anywhere.

E · Inverse video

terminal idiom
For: the app's own vocabulary — the typed-label cursor and the boot sweep already use inverse video, so a swapped fg/bg block is native here and reads instantly. Against: it's the heaviest treatment on the page: two solid blocks in the rail pull more attention than the section headings they control, and it's neither green (state) nor recessed (pressed) — a third vocabulary. Parked for something that must shout (a blocked/armed state), not for latches.

F · Wash only — drop the ring

minimal diff
For: a one-line change — LATCHED_ARM_FLUSH becomes the only arm. Removes the outline the eye snagged on. Against: still additive: a lit pad, not a pressed key. It answers "less border", not "pressed in".

G · Underbar — neutral ink + 2px green bar

exclusive groups
For: the tab idiom — it's what the watched-row underbar already does, and it's the right shape for a one-of-N choice (the surface switcher in switch mode, activity tabs). Against: for an independent any-subset latch (rail, chips) an underbar says "current", not "on". Keep it for exclusive groups where the segments already sit in one bordered wrapper.

2 · Press vs latch — one depth channel

Today's momentary press feedback (the global :active rule, touch's only feedback channel) snaps the background to bg-card — which on dark themes is lighter than the chrome ground. Under a well metaphor that is backwards: a finger pushing a button should make it go down, and a latched button should stay down. Flip the toolbar's press snap to the proposed arm and hold a button: the press becomes a transient well (bg-inset + the well shadow), and latching is "the well stays, and the ink turns green". One channel, two durations.

Ladder under option C′. Hover on a latched control: the ink holds green (hue-stable, as today) and the shade deepens one step — the well analogue of today's 15% → 25% wash step. Focus keeps the green outline at 2px offset; with no ring in the latch there is no longer a second green outline for it to be confused with.
The proposed press snap changes a global rule, not a latch arm — every control on the page would snap into a well on pointer-down. That is the point (one depth channel), but it is a separate decision from the latch shape and should ship as its own change so it can be reverted alone.

3 · Exclusive groups get one key down

An any-subset latch (rail, chips, tile verbs) and a one-of-N choice (surface switcher in switch mode, terminal activity tabs) are different controls wearing the same arm today. In a connected segmented group the well reads best when exactly one segment is down and the rest stay flush with the wrapper — the classic radio-key strip. Below, the same three-segment group under C′ (well) and G (underbar), fine and coarse.

C′ · one segment down

Wrapper owns the border; the down segment sits in the well. The divider between segments stays, so the well has visible walls.

G · underbar

Reads as "current tab". Right when the segments are views of one thing (tty / web / code); wrong when they can be combined.

C′ · toggle mode — a subset may be open

The same wrapper in toggle mode (up to three tiles open): several wells side by side still parse, because each well has its own top shade. An underbar would not scale to this.

5 · Theme derivation — why C works in dark and not in light

ChannelDarkLightConsequence
Ground step, chrome → bg-inset (OKLab ΔL)0.0680.000the well floor is invisible in light; only the shadow is left
On glyph — accent-green on the ground7.6 : 12.7 : 1in light the on glyph is fainter than the off glyph
Off glyph — secondary text on the ground4.5 : 14.0 : 1the hierarchy inverts: pressed reads as washed-out
Bordered controls, border dimmed toward the welldarker → pressedlighter → disabledpressed must go darker in every channel on a light ground

bg-inset is derived per palette by darkening the background a fixed sRGB percentage (5% dark, 6% light) — a step that is visible on one theme category and invisible on the other, the same asymmetry the sidebar-material study measured for the chrome ground. Accent-green is derived as a fill/track colour and on light palettes lands below secondary text in contrast; today's ring has been hiding that. C′ fixes both by derivation, not by hand-picked hexes: floor = chrome ground minus ΔL 0.06 in OKLab (chroma kept), ink = the palette green driven down in OKLab lightness until it clears 3 : 1 on the floor, border = the border token mixed toward secondary text. On pure-black palettes the floor clamps to the ground and the 1px bottom lip keeps the rim.
