Cron Clock — Design Studies

Visual design for docs/specs/cron.md — sidebar placement (the right panel is retired), entry anatomy, the idle-anchored backoff schedule, fire-time target resolution, and the watched-row overlay. All mocks are [target].

1 · Placement — a CLOCK sidebar section + watched rows in the tree

Crons and the watchlist are server-scoped monitoring facts, and the sidebar is run-kit's server-scoped monitoring surface (agent dots, markers, notes live there) — present on the terminal route, costing no navigation and no center real estate. The section-visibility rail is explicitly "the designated home for future sidebar-level controls": CLOCK joins it as a fifth toggle (default off), a standard CollapsiblePanel. The watchlist is deliberately not a separate list — watched workers are already window rows in the tree, so the operator's attention set renders as a row-level indicator, with detail on the row's existing flyout card.

1c · Mobile — a feed, not a registry

The desktop tiers collapse badly on a phone (a 50px drawer panel, flyout-buried mute). Mobile inverts the shape, borrowing from the software people already trust with timers: the Calendar agenda (a time-ordered feed, not a definitions list), the alarm app (one row = one entry with a first-class toggle; edit is its own screen), healthchecks.io (staleness — the dead-man's switch — is the primary status), and PagerDuty (push is the entry point; the app is where you land). The feed lives as an Activity segment on the shipped mobile console sheet — one phone home for talk to the operator and what is its clock doing.

2 · The backoff schedule — a pure function of the idle anchor

The operator tick is duration-based, not wall-clock: fire times are anchor + min·(2ⁿ−1) — +1m, +3m, +7m, +15m… capped at max. The anchor is the agent idle epoch carried by @rk_pane_agent_state — with the anchor-join rule: the effective anchor is the last activity not caused by the clock (idle-epoch resets that immediately follow the clock's own delivery are ignored, by joining against the delivery log). Without the join every delivered tick would reset the raw epoch and pin the ladder at rung 1 forever. No stored counters: both inputs live on disk, a daemon restart re-derives the whole schedule, worst case one duplicate idempotent tick. The entry also carries wake_on: agent-state-change (debounced edge trigger, OR'd in) and suppress_while: [operator-loop-fresh, nothing-tracked] — silent while the in-session /loop is alive or nothing is monitored.

3 · Fire-time target resolution

Entries name durable identities; panes are found at fire time. This is what "kill the operator and the cron continues" means mechanically.

resolution ladder
fire(entry)
├─ target role:operator → pane holding role option → deliver
├─ target sess:4fe2 → pane with @rk_pane_agent_session=4fe2 → deliver
└─ no pane resolves → if_absent:
   ├─ skip → missed fire logged · trends orphaned
   ├─ notify → rk notify (fail-silent)
   └─ respawn → role: rk operator relaunch
                  sess: claude --resume P3
Delivery rides the one injection engine (the communication standard's write channel, agent-messaging.md) — pane-mode guard, echo probe, and submit verification inherited, never raw send-keys. when-idle entries additionally gate on @rk_pane_agent_state. A respawn runs the standard spawn-then-deliver composite: await --ready classifies the fresh pane, a parked wall escalates via notify (the clock never auto-answers walls), delivery proceeds only on ready — and the first delivery is the launcher's kickoff (/fab-operator), never the bare tick: a fresh session has no tick convention in context.
